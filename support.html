Where it came from

I'm Ahmed. I was procrastinating on my own exams, so I did what any reasonable student does and built a flashcard app instead. My girlfriend was studying too, and I wanted us to spend less time making cards and more time learning them.

It worked, so I put it online for everyone.

Five boxes, one idea

Lietner is built on the Leitner system. Five boxes sit on a little 3D desk. Cards you know move up a box and come back less often. Cards you miss go straight back to box 1.

  1. 11 day
  2. 22 days
  3. 34 days
  4. 48 days
  5. 516 days

What you know fades into the background. What you keep forgetting shows up until it sticks.

No card-making

You don't write cards from scratch. The app has a ready-made prompt: paste it into your favourite AI along with your notes or PDF, answer three quick questions, and it writes about 30 cards for you. Paste them back and your deck is ready in minutes.

My promise

  • ✓ It is free, and it will always be free.
  • ✓ It will never have ads.
  • ✓ No account needed. Your cards stay on your device unless you choose to sync.
  • ✓ Open source (MIT), so anyone can check what it does.

Yes, the method is spelled Leitner and I spelled the app Lietner. It's a typo I've decided to keep.

Why I'm asking

Keeping something free and ad-free doesn't make it free to run. Today Lietner fits inside free tiers, but as more students use it, that stops being true. I don't want to solve that with ads or a paywall, because that would break the promise above.
