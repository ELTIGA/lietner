# Lietner — Statement of Intent

Confirmed 2026-10-10. Replaces the earlier version from the same day (personal, desktop-only, no backend).

- **Outcome:** Lietner, a free, MIT-licensed Leitner-system flashcard app on a 3D desk, live at https://lietner.app. Five boxes sit on a desk; a card lifts out, flips to show the answer, then flies to the next box or back to box 1. The same desk works on phone, tablet and desktop. Optional sign-in syncs decks and progress between devices.
- **User:** Students aged 13+ (high school, university, self-learners) studying on their own, mostly on phones. Anyone, of any age, can use every feature except sync as a guest, with no account.
- **Why:** Plain flashcard apps feel flat. Seeing cards move between boxes makes reviewing more fun and makes progress visible. The desk already works on desktop; this turns a personal tool into one any student can use anywhere.
- **Cards:** Plain front/back text in any language or script, including right-to-left. Several decks, added with an in-app form, imported from CSV/TSV (including Anki's plain-text export), or generated by the student's own AI from a copy-paste prompt and pasted back as JSON. Lietner itself never calls an AI.
- **Rules:** Classic Leitner, unchanged. Boxes 1–5 come due every 1/2/4/8/16 days. Self-graded: "Knew it" moves the card up one box, "Missed" sends it back to box 1. Passing box 5 means mastered. Each session reviews all due cards.
- **Accounts:** Optional. Sign in with Google or a 6-digit code sent by email. An account exists only to sync your own decks and progress; nothing is shared or public. Accounts require age 13+ (or the local digital-consent age, up to 16 in parts of the EU).
- **Success:**
  - Public launch with $0/month running cost.
  - A student starts a deck on a laptop, reviews on a phone on the bus, and loses nothing.
  - A 30–50 card session feels fast on every device: animations can be skipped or sped up, with keyboard shortcuts on desktop and taps and swipes on touch screens.
  - 30 days after launch, 100 students study weekly.
- **Constraints:**
  - $0/month until donations fund Supabase Pro ($25/month). The only fixed cost is the yearly domain renewal.
  - Solo maintainer: a static site on Vercel Hobby plus Supabase (auth, Postgres with row-level security). No server code beyond SQL migrations: row-level security policies, constraints, triggers and one account-deletion function.
  - Local-first: IndexedDB stays the source of truth and the server is a sync mirror, so the app keeps working offline or while the backend is paused. JSON export/import stays.
- **Out of scope for launch:** Sharing, classes or a public deck library; images, math, audio or Markdown on cards; reminders and push notifications; stats and streaks; native apps; under-13 accounts; UI languages other than English; mainland China; in-app AI generation (calling an AI from Lietner); configurable boxes or intervals; typed-answer checking; native `.apkg` import.
- **Accepted risks:**
  - No beta. A real-device pass and data-loss drills stand in for testers.
  - No server backups until Supabase Pro.
  - Email sign-in caps at 100 codes/day on the free email plan. Above that, people are pointed to Google sign-in or guest mode.
  - The free Supabase project pauses after a week without database activity. Sync stops until it's resumed; the app keeps working.
